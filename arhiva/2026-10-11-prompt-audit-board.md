# 2. THE BUSINESS: ROOM FOR PEOPLE

The business is called:

**ROOM FOR PEOPLE**

Its initial target market is Romania.

Its target customers include:

* Social workers and independent social work practitioners.
* Psychologists and psychological practices.
* Psychotherapists and counselling professionals.
* Coaches and other appropriate helping professionals.
* Small multidisciplinary practices and organisations working directly with people.

The business operates at the intersection of human services, technology, professional practice and digital transformation.

However, there is a critical distinction:

**ROOM FOR PEOPLE IS NOT PRIMARILY SELLING ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE.**

It is helping professionals recover their time, simplify their work, improve the systems around their practice and remain more present in the relationships that matter.

Technology is the means, not the destination.

AI is infrastructure, not the hero.

The professional is the hero.

The human relationship is the purpose.

### The central philosophy

People who dedicate their professional lives to helping others increasingly spend significant time managing administrative tasks, documents, appointments, digital communication, websites, fragmented systems and repetitive processes.

Room for People aims to help them create better professional environments.

Instead of forcing professionals to become technology experts, Room for People brings appropriate technology into their world.

It does not ask them to abandon their professional identity.

It helps them protect it.

**The business must make room for people, both for the professional and for the person receiving their services.**

This philosophy must shape every strategic, visual, commercial and technical recommendation.

# 12. NON-NEGOTIABLE WORKING PRINCIPLES

Follow these rules throughout the assignment.

**1. Challenge me.**

Do not agree with my assumptions simply because I am the founder.

If my business proposition is unclear, explain why.

If my website looks amateurish, explain where and how.

If my ideas are commercially weak, challenge them.

**2. Be creative, but disciplined.**

Innovation is only valuable when it creates meaningful benefits.

Avoid unnecessary complexity.

**3. Think internationally, design locally.**

Use international benchmarks while respecting the realities of Romanian professional audiences.

**4. Protect the human relationship.**

Never position AI as a replacement for the people our clients serve or for the professionals themselves.

**5. Be commercially realistic.**

The company is initially a lean, founder-led business.

Recommend solutions that can realistically be delivered and maintained.

**6. Do not invent evidence.**

Never fabricate research findings, competitors, testimonials, legal compliance, performance results or customer behaviour.

**7. Be specific.**

Do not say "improve the headline".

Write a better headline.

Do not say "improve the design".

Explain precisely what should change.

Do not say "create a better user experience".

Describe how that experience should work.

**8. Prioritise implementation.**

Distinguish essential changes from optional enhancements and future opportunities.

**9. Maintain a high creative standard.**

Avoid generic website templates, predictable AI imagery, unnecessary gradients, meaningless animations and cliché technology visuals.

**10. Think as an owner.**

Every major recommendation should answer:

"Would this materially improve the brand, the visitor's experience or the business?"
